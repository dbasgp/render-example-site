Technology

Three principles.
Every climate solved.

Choosing the right dehumidifier starts with understanding how it removes water from air. Each method excels in different conditions.

01

Refrigerant (compressor)

The workhorse for everyday climates. Air is drawn over a cold evaporator coil — moisture condenses, falls into a collection system, and dry air is returned to the room.

  • Best for ambient temperatures 15–35°C
  • Highest extraction per kWh in warm, humid air
  • Used in all DBA mobile, ceiling, and industrial models
02

Desiccant (rotary)

A silica honeycomb wheel actively absorbs water vapour — even at sub-zero temperatures where compressors stall. Continuous, precise, low-temp drying.

  • Operates from −20°C to 40°C
  • Maintains RH 1–90% with ±3% precision
  • Cold storage, pharma, lithium battery rooms, lyophilisation
03

Humidistat (dehumidify + humidify)

Hybrid units that hold a target RH band by removing or adding moisture as needed. Wet-membrane evaporative humidification, isenthalpic and clean.

  • Two-way control around a setpoint (e.g. 50–55% RH)
  • Auto self-cleaning water tank, RS485 BMS integration
  • Galleries, museums, wine cellars, data centres

Questions

Everything you might want to know.

What's the difference between a refrigerant and a desiccant dehumidifier?

A refrigerant unit cools incoming air past its dew point — water condenses and is drained. It's most efficient in warm, humid air (15–35°C). A desiccant unit uses a silica wheel that adsorbs water vapour directly, so it works at low temperatures (down to −20°C) where refrigerant coils would frost over. For a typical home in a tropical climate, a refrigerant unit is the right choice. For cold rooms, pharma, or precision labs, choose desiccant.

How big a unit do I need for my space?

As a rule of thumb, a well-insulated home needs 0.5–1 L/day per 10 sq ft to hold a comfortable 50–60% RH. Heavy moisture loads (basements, indoor pools, post-flood drying) can demand 5–10× more. The sizing table above is a starting reference — share your floor plan and we'll size it precisely.

What humidity should I aim for?

For human comfort and to discourage mould and dust mites, 50–60% RH is the sweet spot. Below 40% the air starts to feel dry; above 65% you risk condensation, mould growth, and dust-mite proliferation. Galleries and wine cellars typically target 50–55% with tighter ±2% control.

Do ceiling-mount units need ductwork?

Yes — ceiling units use short-run ductwork to distribute dry air through a void above the ceiling, with grilles in each zone. This lets one unit serve multiple rooms quietly. Slim-line models (UTC series) install in just 200 mm of plenum.

How loud are these units?

Modern DBA ceiling units run 38–48 dB at 1 m — about as loud as a quiet conversation, and noticeably quieter than a portable air conditioner. Mobile units sit around 48–55 dB. Industrial floor units are louder by design and best installed in plant rooms.

Can I integrate units with my building management system?

Yes. Most DBA commercial models include RS485 with Modbus, allowing direct integration with BMS platforms for centralised setpoint control, alarms, and runtime telemetry.
