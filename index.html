Technology

Three principles.
Every climate solved.

Choosing the right dehumidifier starts with understanding how it removes water from air. Each method excels in different conditions.

01

Refrigerant (compressor)

The workhorse for everyday tropical climates. Air is drawn over a cold evaporator coil — moisture condenses, falls into a collection system, and dry air is returned to the room.

  • Best for ambient temperatures 5–40°C
  • Highest extraction per kWh in warm, humid air
  • Used across UTC, GEC, GE, GEX, and DH product lines
02

Desiccant (rotary)

A silica honeycomb wheel actively adsorbs water vapour — even at sub-zero temperatures where compressors stall. Continuous, precise, low-temperature drying.

  • Operates from −20°C to 40°C
  • Maintains RH 1–90% with high precision
  • Cold storage, pharma, lithium battery rooms, lyophilisation
03

Humidity Control Unit

Hybrid units that hold a target RH band by removing or adding moisture as needed. Wet-membrane evaporative humidification, isenthalpic and clean.

  • Two-way control around a setpoint (e.g. 50–55% RH)
  • UV-C sterilisation, RS485 BMS integration
  • Galleries, museums, wine cellars, data centres

Questions

Everything you might want to know.

What is the difference between a refrigerant and a desiccant dehumidifier?

A refrigerant unit cools incoming air past its dew point — water condenses and is drained. It is most efficient in warm, humid air (5–40°C) and is the right choice for tropical Singapore homes and offices. A desiccant unit uses a silica wheel that adsorbs water vapour directly, so it works at low temperatures (down to −20°C) where refrigerant coils would frost over. Choose desiccant for cold rooms, pharma, lithium-battery production, or precision laboratories.

How big a unit do I need for my space?

As a rule of thumb, a well-insulated home needs 0.5–1 L/day per 10 sq ft to hold a comfortable 50–60% RH. Heavy moisture loads (basements, indoor pools, post-flood drying) can demand 5–10× more. The sizing table above is a starting reference — share your floor plan and we will size it precisely.

What humidity should I aim for?

For human comfort and to discourage mould and dust mites, 50–60% RH is the sweet spot. Below 40% the air starts to feel dry; above 65% you risk condensation, mould growth, and dust-mite proliferation. Galleries and wine cellars typically target 50–55% with tighter ±2% control via the HC humidity-control series.

Do ceiling-mount units need ductwork?

Yes — ceiling units use short-run ductwork to distribute dry air through a void above the ceiling, with grilles in each zone. This lets one unit serve multiple rooms quietly. Ultra-slim UTC models install in just 200–310 mm of plenum height.

How loud are these units?

UTC ceiling units run 39–50 dB at 3 m — quieter than a quiet conversation, and noticeably quieter than a typical portable air conditioner. Commercial GEC ceiling units sit at 49–65 dB. Industrial floor-standing GE units are louder by design and best installed in plant rooms.

Can I integrate units with my building management system?

Yes. Every UTC, GEC, GE, HC and DD model includes RS485 with Modbus, allowing direct integration with BMS platforms for centralised setpoint control, alarms, and runtime telemetry. Smaller UTC and GEC68 models also offer WiFi App control.

Can a dehumidifier replace my air conditioner?

No — but it complements one. An air conditioner cools and removes some moisture as a byproduct. A dehumidifier removes moisture far more efficiently per kWh and lets you run your AC at higher temperatures while still feeling cool, because dry air feels cooler. In tropical climates, running both together typically saves energy versus running an AC alone.

What does "LGR" mean in the GEX series?

LGR stands for Low-Grain Refrigerant — a design that pre-cools incoming air before the evaporator, allowing extraction at lower humidity levels than standard refrigerant units. LGR is the standard for restoration and water-damage recovery, where you need to drive a room down to 30–35% RH in a hurry.
